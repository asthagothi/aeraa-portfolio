Three art directions, each continuing straight down from the existing About Me page in one continuous scroll. Drag the scroll slider under each scene to see how its key transition would move. All pins are clearly marked placeholders until the real pin images are supplied.

Recommendation: Direction 2, “The Board Assembles”, with Direction 1’s editorial typography

Page 1 is built from interface objects floating in a photo studio: the intro card, the phone-call widget, the skills panel and the browser bar, all over the portrait on the grey wall. Direction 2 carries that language straight into Pinterest. The portrait becomes the first pin, the pins themselves are the floating objects, the analytics arrive as a phone-style widget, and the Page 1 browser bar becomes the affiliate destination.

It also reuses what is already built and tested (GSAP + ScrollTrigger and the floating-card system), so it adds no new dependencies. Pinning is limited to two short scenes, the board forming and the affiliate journey, so scrolling never feels trapped.

Figures in these mocks are placeholders. The analytics screenshot shows 3.7l impressions, 17t engagements, 33 outbound clicks, 3.2t saves, 3l total audience and 14t engaged audience for 20/09/26 – 20/10/26. The “l / t” suffixes look like lakh and thousand, so 3.7l would be about 370,000. That needs confirming before anything is published.

Look

The same grey studio wall, white display type and floating UI objects as Page 1. Page 2 reads as the next scene in the same world.

Pinterest content

In a pinned scene, the portrait shrinks into the first pin while your real pins fly in from the edges and settle into a masonry board. The board then releases into a normal scrolling gallery with links to the original pins.

Analytics

A dark phone-style widget, the sibling of Page 1’s call widget, slides up over the board. It shows the historical figures with their date range and notes the difference between impressions, saves and outbound clicks.

Affiliate

A second short pinned scene zooms into one pin: idea → product → link. Page 1’s browser bar then appears showing your Amazon link, with the disclosure directly beside it.

Transitions

In: the portrait shrinks into a pin, which is the strongest possible tie to Page 1. Out: the board’s pins line up into a vertical video frame, a cue for “art in motion” and Page 3.

Advantages

The most continuous with Page 1, and memorable. It reuses the existing scroll engine and card system, and the visuals lead.

Drawbacks

The most complex option. Pinned scenes need careful tuning on mobile, so phones get a simplified, unpinned version. It also needs real pin images to fully land.

Generous negative space, one statement at a time, single large images, and thin lines as the only ornament.

Affiliate

A four-step line that draws itself as you scroll, lighting each step: pin → idea → link → product.

Transition from Page 1

The hero fades out while the grey stays constant, and the first statement rises into the space it leaves.

Link to Page 1

Shares the palette and type, but deliberately quieter than Page 1’s busy composition.

Advantages

Calm, fast, highly accessible, and easy to maintain.

Drawbacks

Undersells a visual body of work. Pinterest is about abundance, and one image at a time works against that. It can also feel like a different site from Page 1.

Shared across all three

  • Single continuous scroll: Page 1 stays exactly as it is, and Page 2 begins where the placeholder “Selected Work” demo currently sits.
  • Only real figures are shown, labelled “historical” with their date range. Monthly views, impressions and outbound clicks stay distinct, and nothing implies sales or earnings.
  • The affiliate disclosure sits next to every affiliate link, and there are no links until your real URLs are supplied.
  • Reduced motion and mobile get a calm, unpinned version with all content visible.
